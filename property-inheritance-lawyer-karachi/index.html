Property and inheritance

Property and inheritance disputes in Karachi

How inherited property is divided, how possession is recovered, and what the courts actually require. Written for the person in the dispute, not for other lawyers.

Book a consultation Call +92 348 3996102

By Noman Sohail, Advocate High Court, Karachi. Last reviewed November 2026.

A brother who will not divide the family house. A flat sold on a power of attorney nobody saw. A plot occupied while the owner was working abroad. These are the property disputes that fill the civil courts of Karachi, and each has a defined legal route. This page sets out those routes plainly, so that you know what you are dealing with before you instruct anyone.

Inherited property that is not being divided

Three principles settle most of the confusion.

  • Inheritance opens at death. The heirs become owners of their shares at that moment, automatically. No court order, transfer or mutation is needed to create the right.
  • Shares are fixed by personal law. For Muslims they are set by Islamic law according to the deceased’s school. They cannot be altered by the wish of the family member who happens to be in possession.
  • Records follow title, not the other way round. A mutation or a society transfer in one heir’s name does not extinguish the others’ shares.

The correct procedure then depends on whether the heirs agree.

Where all heirs agree

For bank accounts, shares, savings certificates and similar assets, the heirs need a succession certificate. For immovable property they need letters of administration. Under the Sindh Letters of Administration and Succession Certificates Act 2021, undisputed cases can be processed through NADRA’s Succession Facilitation Units on biometric verification of the heirs, without a court case. Where that route is unavailable, the application is made to the court under the Succession Act 1925.

Where an heir is refusing, delaying or denying

The remedy is a suit for administration and partition. In substance the court is asked to do five things:

  1. Declare who the legal heirs are and what each share is.
  2. Identify the whole estate, including assets one heir may be concealing.
  3. Take accounts of rent and income collected by the heir in possession, and of any debts of the deceased.
  4. Divide the property physically where that is practical.
  5. Where it is not, as with a single house or flat, order sale and distribute the proceeds according to shares. A co-heir is usually given the chance to buy out the others first.

The court first passes a preliminary decree settling heirs and shares, then a final decree after accounts and partition. In Karachi the sale is normally conducted through the court’s own officer.

The first application matters as much as the suit

The real danger in these cases is the property being sold, gifted or encumbered while the case is pending. An application for an interim injunction, filed with the suit, asks the court to restrain any transfer until the shares are decided. A transfer made during the suit is in any event subject to its outcome.

What about delay?

People often assume a share is lost because years have passed. The courts have long treated possession by one co-heir as possession on behalf of all, so mere delay in asking for a share does not by itself defeat it. The position changes where the other side relies on a specific document, such as a gift deed or relinquishment, and you have known about it for years. Then limitation becomes a live issue. The date you first learned of the adverse document is one of the first things I ask about.

Someone is occupying your property

There are three routes. Which one fits depends on how recently you were dispossessed and what documents you hold.

RouteWhereWhat it does
Illegal Dispossession Act 2005Sessions Court, by direct complaintCriminal proceedings against the person who took the property. The court can call a police inquiry, grant interim relief and order possession restored. It is not confined to organised land grabbers.
Section 9, Specific Relief Act 1877Civil CourtA quick suit to recover possession from anyone who dispossessed you without due process. Title is not examined. It must be filed within six months of the dispossession.
Suit for possession on titleCivil Court, or the High Court by valueThe full remedy: declaration of ownership, possession, permanent injunction and mesne profits for the period of wrongful occupation.

These are not mutually exclusive, and in serious cases more than one is used. What matters at the start is proof of two things: your title or lawful possession, and the date and manner in which you lost it.

A sale agreement that was not honoured

If you paid under an agreement to sell and the seller will not complete, the remedy is a suit for specific performance. The court will want to see that you were always ready and willing to pay the balance, and it commonly directs the buyer to deposit that balance in court at an early stage. The limitation period is generally three years from the date fixed for performance or, where no date was fixed, from when you learned that performance was refused. Waiting in the hope that the seller will come round is the most common way good claims are lost.

If you are the seller and the buyer defaulted, the agreement does not simply lapse. You may need a formal notice and, where the buyer holds a document that clouds your title, a suit for its cancellation.

Builders, societies and record problems

Delayed possession by a builder, double allotment by a society, a transfer refused by an authority, or an entry wrongly changed in the record each has its own forum: the civil court, the consumer court, the relevant authority and its appellate hierarchy, or the High Court of Sindh in its constitutional jurisdiction where a public body has acted unlawfully. Choosing the wrong forum costs months. This is usually the first question I resolve on reading the papers.

If you live abroad

A large share of Karachi property disputes involve an owner or heir who lives overseas, and the people on the ground know it. You can act through an attorney appointed by a power of attorney attested at the Pakistani mission in your country. Keep the scope of that document narrow and specific to the litigation: broad general powers of attorney are themselves a frequent source of property fraud.

What to bring to a first consultation

  • Title documents: lease, sale deed, allotment or transfer letter, and the latest search or record extract if you have one
  • For inheritance matters: death certificate, the NADRA family registration certificate, and CNIC copies of the heirs
  • Any agreement to sell, receipts and proof of payment
  • Any gift deed, relinquishment, power of attorney or mutation the other side relies on
  • Utility bills, tax receipts or tenancy papers showing who has been in possession
  • Every notice, court paper or police paper already exchanged, with dates

You do not need all of these to start. Bring what you have, and a short written timeline.

Common questions

Short answers to what people ask first. Every case turns on its own documents, so treat these as orientation, not advice on your matter.

How long does an inheritance or partition case take in Karachi?

There is no honest single figure. An uncontested succession certificate or letters of administration can be completed in weeks to a few months. A contested administration suit, where one heir disputes shares or possession, commonly runs for years through evidence and appeal. What can be obtained early is interim protection: an order stopping sale or transfer of the property while the suit is pending.

Can one heir sell the whole inherited property without the others?

No. Each heir becomes owner of a defined share at the moment of death. One heir can deal only with that share. A sale of the entire property by a single heir does not bind the shares of the others, and the buyer steps into the shoes of the selling heir only. The other heirs can sue for a declaration, cancellation of the document to the extent of their shares, and partition.

The property was transferred to my brother's name in the records. Have I lost my share?

Not by that fact alone. Mutation and society or authority records are maintained for administrative and fiscal purposes. They are evidence, but they do not create or extinguish ownership. If the transfer rests on a gift, relinquishment or power of attorney that you dispute, the person relying on that document has to prove it, and courts look closely at such documents where they deprive other heirs.

My sisters or mother are being denied their share. What does the law say?

A woman's share in inheritance arises by operation of law at the moment of death and does not depend on anyone's permission. Courts treat alleged relinquishments by female heirs with caution and require clear proof that they were voluntary and understood. Depriving a woman of her inheritance by deceitful or illegal means is also a criminal offence under section 498-A of the Pakistan Penal Code.

I live abroad. Can I pursue a property case in Karachi without travelling?

In most cases, yes. You can appoint an attorney in Pakistan through a power of attorney executed abroad and attested by the Pakistani embassy or consulate, which is then completed in Pakistan. The attorney can file and pursue the case. There are limits on what an attorney can depose to from personal knowledge, so the evidence stage needs planning from the start.

Is a succession certificate enough to transfer a house?

No. A succession certificate covers debts and securities: bank accounts, shares, certificates and similar movable assets. Immovable property is dealt with through letters of administration, or through a decree in a suit where there is a dispute. Where all heirs agree, the NADRA Succession Facilitation Unit route under the Sindh law of 2021 can be used for both.

Someone has occupied my plot. Should I go to the police or the court?

Usually the court. Police treat most possession disputes as civil. The Illegal Dispossession Act 2005 allows a complaint directly before the Sessions Court, which can order an inquiry and restore possession. A civil suit for possession may be needed alongside it or instead of it, depending on your documents and how long ago the dispossession occurred. Do not attempt to retake possession by force.

Have the papers read before you decide anything

Most property disputes are decided by documents and dates. A first consultation is a structured reading of what you hold, an honest view of where you stand, and the realistic options with their cost and duration.
